## I. The Allocation Problem at the Inflection

When the Transformer paper was submitted in June 2017, every technically literate reader who attempted to assess its implications was performing, whether they recognized it or not, a signal-processing problem under channel capacity constraint. The paper was not a single signal. It was a compound signal: an architectural result, a hardware chronology, a training data implication, a benchmark consequence, a scaling trajectory, and — embedded in the hardware section and the architecture's sequence generality — a cross-domain implication that the paper's authors did not name and that the credential market's signal apparatus could not detect. Each component carried different information value depending on the reader's objective function. Which components received channel capacity determined which inferences were available.

Shannon's (1948) coding theorem establishes the relevant constraint. No information channel transmits at a rate exceeding its capacity, regardless of the sophistication of either the signal or the recipient. Sims (2003) imported this result into economics with a specific formal consequence: the mutual information between the economic environment's state and the agent's posterior beliefs is bounded by a capacity parameter κ. This is not a metaphor for cognitive overload. It is a derivable constraint on any information-processing system, biological or institutional, that the economics curriculum makes explicit as the central object of rational inattention theory.

The credential-optimized CS student's channel capacity allocation was fully determined before the Transformer paper appeared. Three to five years of domain-specific optimization had tuned their attention toward algorithmic innovation, benchmark results, framework conventions, and the assessment signals that the institutional labor market rewarded. This allocation was individually rational under credential optimization's objective function. For that objective, the highest-value components of the Transformer paper were the BLEU improvement and the architectural novelty. The hardware section and the sequence generality's cross-domain implication were near-zero value: they did not change the optimal action for 2017–2022 credential building. The RI-optimal allocation for the credential objective correctly placed near-zero channel capacity on the paper's most consequential components for the long run.

Maćkowiak, Matějka, and Wiederholt's (2023) comprehensive review of the rational inattention literature confirms the general structure: agents allocate processing capacity proportionally to value-weighted information content — the product of a signal's information value and the probability that attending to it changes the agent's action in a way that matters for their objective. Two agents with different objective functions will, both behaving RI-optimally, extract entirely different information from the same compound signal. The difference is not intelligence. It is the objective function, and the objective function is shaped by the training.

The economics-trained outlier at a technical institution had a different objective function: long-run portfolio allocation of developmental investment across the post-AI labor market. For that objective, the value-weighted information content ranking of the Transformer paper's components is inverted. The hardware section is the most valuable component: it establishes that scaling at the frontier is newly compute-feasible, bounding the timeline. The sequence generality is the second most valuable: it establishes the architecture's training distribution will expand to include code, which the GitHub corpus makes available at scale. The benchmark result is confirmatory: it validates the scaling trajectory empirically. The BLEU score is near-zero information value for the long-run objective — it confirms that NLP translation improved, which is irrelevant to the labor market inference.

This is not a retrospective rationalization. It is the direct application of a theoretical framework the economics outlier had already studied to a decision problem that was live at the time. Hébert and Woodford (2021) formalize the relevant case: when decisions take time and information arrives sequentially, the RI-optimal agent commits in advance to a signal weighting scheme calibrated to their objective before seeing the full information environment. The developmental investment decision — which field to study, which signals to calibrate attention toward, which inferences to hold open — is exactly this type of prior commitment. The economics outlier's prior commitment to long-run portfolio thinking over short-run credential optimization was not foresight about AI specifically. It was the prior commitment that economics training instills: treat the allocation of attention as itself an optimization problem, with the objective function stated explicitly before the signals arrive.

The credential machine's most consequential failure was not producing Python graduates at peak throughput during the obsolescence window, though it did that. It was producing Python graduates whose prior commitment — calibrated by every assessment, every curriculum choice, every institutional reward — was to short-run credential optimization. Under that prior, the RI-optimal reader of the Transformer paper extracts exactly the information that 2017–2022 required and exactly none of the information that 2022–2026 revealed to be consequential. The machine optimized the prior into the wrong shape, and the prior determined the allocation, and the allocation determined the inference, and the inference was never completed.

## II. The Softmax at the Foundation of Economics and Architecture

The multinomial logit model is one of the most widely estimated choice models in applied economics. It predicts the probability that an agent selects alternative *a* from a choice set, given state *s*, according to a softmax distribution over the alternatives' utilities. For four decades after its introduction, the logit's theoretical foundation was understood as a convenient assumption about the distribution of idiosyncratic preference shocks: Gumbel-distributed noise added to each alternative's utility produces the logit probabilities as a distributional consequence.

Matějka and McKay (2015) revised this understanding entirely. Their result establishes that the multinomial logit is not a preference shock assumption. It is the optimal choice rule for an agent who faces a Shannon channel capacity constraint and must choose optimally how to process information about the available alternatives before acting. The RI-optimal policy satisfies:

**P\*(a | s) ∝ λ(a) · exp(U(a, s) / μ)**

where *μ* is the shadow price of the information constraint — the marginal cost of one unit of mutual information — and λ(*a*) is the prior probability of action *a*. When the prior is uniform, this reduces to pure softmax: the multinomial logit is rational inattention under an information cost.

The Transformer's core attention mechanism, as specified in Vaswani et al. (2017), implements:

**Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QK^T / √d_k) · V**

The correspondence is not analogical. It is formal. The softmax in the Transformer's attention mechanism and the softmax in the Matějka-McKay RI optimal policy are the same mathematical object, instantiated in different notation and derived from different starting premises, converging on the same solution because they are both solving the same underlying optimization problem: allocate processing resources across a distribution of signals proportionally to their relative value, with temperature governing the sharpness of the allocation.

The mapping: queries *Q* are the current state from which the agent is attending; keys *K* are the set of actions or items to potentially attend to; *U(a, s)* corresponds to the inner product *QK^T* capturing the relevance of each key given the current query; *μ* corresponds to √*d_k*, the temperature that normalizes the information cost; and the weighted retrieval *softmax(·) · V* corresponds to the expected-utility-weighted action that the RI agent takes after allocating their probability mass according to the optimal policy. The Transformer implements constant-time rational inattention across the sequence: at every position, the architecture computes the RI-optimal allocation of processing resources across all prior positions, weighted by relevance, normalized by the information cost parameter.

The American Economic Review published this result in 2015. The Transformer paper appeared in 2017. The economics literature had formally derived the mathematical foundation of the Transformer's core innovation — from first principles, in a different domain, two years before the architecture appeared. This was not a coincidence of form. Both results were solving the same problem: how to allocate finite processing resources across a distribution of signals when the cost of processing is proportional to the mutual information extracted. The RI framework solved it for economic choice; the Transformer implemented it for sequence processing. The mathematical structure is identical because the optimization problem is identical.

An economics student who had encountered the Sims-to-Matějka-McKay line of work — from the channel capacity constraint to the discrete choice theorem — was, when they read the Transformer paper's attention mechanism, recognizing a formal acquaintance in new notation. Not approximately. The architecture they were reading had independently derived, from engineering constraints, the same optimal policy that economics had derived from rational behavior constraints. This recognition generates a category of prediction unavailable to the reader who encounters the attention mechanism as architectural novelty: not merely "this works" but "this generalizes because it must, for structural reasons that hold across any domain where an information-processing agent allocates limited attention across a distribution of signals weighted by relevance." The domain-neutrality of the information-theoretic foundation is the reason the architecture generalizes to code, to mathematics, to reasoning — and the economics student who knew the foundation knew the generalization before it was demonstrated.

Caplin and Dean (2015) extend the RI framework to reveal that any observed choice behavior consistent with RI can be characterized by a unique information cost function — the cost the agent is implicitly treating as the price of attention. The Transformer's temperature parameter √*d_k* is exactly this: the implicit price of attention, set by architectural choice rather than behavioral inference, but functionally identical. An economics student trained in revealed preference and RI would recognize the temperature parameter not as a scaling trick for numerical stability — the explanation in the Transformer paper — but as the information cost that governs the sharpness of the attention allocation and determines which signals dominate the processing.

The Morris-Shin (2002) result amplifies this advantage at the population level. When agents face dispersed private information and receive a common public signal, each agent's rational response — under any Bayesian updating rule — is to overweight the public signal relative to its intrinsic information content, because the public signal's value as a coordination device is added to its value as a signal about fundamentals. The Python credential, in 2017–2022, was the public signal of maximum coordination value: visible to every employer, legible to every hiring manager, reinforced by every institutional recommendation. Morris and Shin predict that a population of agents receiving this signal will systematically overallocate attention to it relative to the signal's fundamental information value — precisely because its public nature produces a coordination multiplier that inflates the apparent value of attending to it.

The economics student trained in the Morris-Shin result held the formal mechanism for discounting the overpriced public signal. Their training had produced the explicit diagnostic: when public signals are common knowledge and agents' payoffs depend on others' actions, the market systematically overvalues public signals and undervalues private signals. The cross-domain scaling patterns in THE-DEDUCTION-WINDOW — the hardware chronology, the sequence generality inference, the teachability-substitutability connection — were private signals in exactly this sense: not visible to the market, not legible to institutional evaluation, not coordinating anyone's action and therefore not benefiting from the Morris-Shin coordination multiplier. They were underpriced by exactly the amount the credential signal was overpriced. The economics student's formal apparatus told them to demand a higher weight on the underpriced private signals. Not as contrarianism — as RI-optimal behavior under a known market failure.

## III. Adaptive Learning and the Regime-Change Prior

Evans and Honkapohja's (2001) foundational treatment of learning in macroeconomics establishes the structure of belief dynamics in economies where agents do not begin at rational expectations equilibrium but must learn their way toward it through recursive updating. Their central concept — E-stability — is the condition under which adaptive learning converges to the rational expectations equilibrium: the condition that, when agents update their beliefs in the direction of the forecast errors generated by the current belief, the updating process has a stable fixed point at the RE equilibrium. E-stability holds under a wide range of conditions in stable environments. Its status in regime-change environments is the critical question for understanding the 2017–2026 window.

The distinction between Bayesian optimal learning and constant-gain learning is the mechanism. A Bayesian optimal learner weights the entire history of observations according to their posterior probability: earlier observations receive their full evidential weight, and the agent converges to the true parameter values at the optimal statistical rate in a stable environment. A constant-gain learner applies a fixed positive weight to each new observation and discounts the old at a constant exponential rate: this is suboptimal in stable environments (the learner never fully exploits the long history) and optimal in regime-change environments (the learner is not anchored to the pre-change history when the structural break arrives). The gain parameter governs the tradeoff: high gain means faster adaptation to new regimes, lower efficiency in stable ones.

The 2017–2026 window was a regime-change environment. The structural break — the AI transition from NLP benchmark improvement to code generation saturation — is precisely documented across the ERIE lineage. In a regime-change environment, the agent whose learning dynamics are closest to constant-gain — whose prior is not fully committed to the pre-break model — adapts fastest when the break arrives. The economics discipline trains its practitioners in constant-gain learning as a professional default: no macroeconomic model is treated as permanent, every major paper challenges the current consensus, and the appropriate model for each phenomenon is itself an empirical question whose answer changes with evidence. This is not epistemological weakness. It is the learning dynamics that the adaptive learning literature identifies as optimal under the environmental uncertainty that the AI transition represented.

Panu Molavi's (2019) general theory deepens the account. Molavi shows that agents who maintain explicit uncertainty about their model class — who retain prior probability mass on models other than their current best-fit model — exhibit systematically better performance in regime-change environments because the structural break has less distance to travel before becoming the dominant model in their posterior. The formal result: model-uncertainty-aware learners have lower updating inertia at the regime boundary, and their expected performance over the full pre-and-post-break period dominates Bayesian optimal learners' performance whenever the probability of a structural break within the evaluation horizon is above a threshold that depends on the break's magnitude and the historical sample size.

The economics discipline is structurally organized around model uncertainty in a way that generates the Molavi-optimal learner as its default graduate. The field maintains active competing frameworks — New Keynesian DSGE, agent-based models, behavioral macro, institutional economics — with no single paradigm claiming definitive authority over the full range of phenomena. A practitioner trained in economics cannot answer "which model?" by reference to disciplinary authority: they must assess each application on its merits, retaining prior mass on alternatives that the current best-fit model might not capture. This is the Molavi model-uncertainty-aware learner: explicitly uncertain about model class, updating faster at regime boundaries, holding the alternative model alive in the posterior until the evidence forces a decision.

The CS, mathematics, physics, and applied mathematics curricula each train their practitioners toward the opposite: within-paradigm depth rather than between-paradigm uncertainty. A CS student trained in computational complexity theory has a model of computation that is treated as foundational — not uncertain, not potentially misspecified, not one of several competing frameworks for understanding algorithmic behavior. A mathematics student trained in proof theory has a model of valid inference that is treated as correct — not a possible framework, but the framework. The practitioner who is deepest in their paradigm is the practitioner with the highest updating inertia when the regime changes: they have the most historical evidence committed to the pre-break model, and the Bayesian update required to shift to the post-break model is the largest.

Branch and Evans's (2006) heterogeneous expectations framework completes the adaptive learning account at the population level. In their model, agents choose among competing forecasting models based on recent predictive performance, with market share of each model evolving through selection pressure. Near structural breaks, the model distribution is heterogeneous: some agents have already shifted toward the post-break model; others remain anchored to the pre-break paradigm. The agents who update earliest earn the highest returns during the transition. The economics outlier was the Branch-Evans early mover: the agent whose prior included the post-AI world as a non-negligible possibility before the empirical confirmation arrived, because their model class had never foreclosed it.

What forecloses the alternative model is the professional cost of maintaining it. In a field where the dominant paradigm is treated as foundational, entertaining an alternative is professionally costly — it reads as ignorance of the established framework rather than principled uncertainty about its domain of validity. CS students who in 2017 expressed uncertainty about whether programming credentials would retain value were expressing ignorance of the labor market's established signal function, not prescient model uncertainty. The economics student entertaining the same uncertainty was applying a standard professional operation — model uncertainty as default, not exception — to a new domain. The professional cost was identical. The prior that generated the uncertainty was different.

The Sargent (1999) account of the conquest of American inflation through boundedly rational learning provides the historical template. Sargent shows that the Federal Reserve's learning dynamics — specifically, its use of a misspecified Phillips curve model as the basis for policy — produced the Great Inflation of the 1970s and its subsequent resolution as the institution updated its model in response to accumulating evidence. The lesson: institutional learning dynamics determine macro outcomes, and the dynamics are governed by the model class maintained and the gain parameter applied. The credential machine's institutional learning dynamics — the enrollment decisions, curriculum investments, and "learn to code" cultural outputs that peaked in 2019–2022 — were the Sargent Great Inflation of the credential market: a policy anchored to a model that had already been structurally broken, sustained by institutional inertia until the evidence was undeniable, with the costs falling on the graduates produced during the peak of the error.

## IV. The Import Structure of Economics and the Technical Outlier

In 1947, Paul Samuelson published *Foundations of Economic Analysis* with the explicit ambition of establishing economics as a formal science whose mathematical structure was homologous to classical mechanics. The foundations he chose were thermodynamic: equilibrium conditions as saddle points of variational problems, comparative statics as perturbation theory, the stability analysis of economic equilibria as the analog of classical mechanical stability. Samuelson was transparent about the import: the mathematical apparatus of physics was being applied to economic phenomena not because the two domains were ontologically identical, but because the formal structures — optimization under constraint, equilibrium as an extremum, perturbation analysis — were structurally homologous in ways that made the translation productive.

This import established a precedent and a methodology that has governed economics ever since. John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern imported the axiomatic method and the theory of combinatorial games from mathematics, creating game theory as an economics science. Kenneth Arrow and Gérard Debreu imported Brouwer's fixed-point theorem and Bourbaki's abstract functional analysis, establishing the existence of general equilibrium under minimal assumptions. Fischer Black and Myron Scholes imported the heat equation — a partial differential equation from classical physics describing diffusion processes — and derived the option pricing formula. Claude Shannon's information theory, developed for communication engineering, was imported by Christopher Sims into macroeconomics to model agents' information processing constraints. Each import followed the same pattern: a mathematical object or result developed in one domain was recognized, by an economist with sufficient cross-domain structural literacy, as formally applicable to an economic phenomenon that the existing economics vocabulary could not fully capture.

The economics discipline's distinctive feature is not that it imports from other fields — every advanced discipline borrows methods at its frontier. It is that the import is systematic, canonical, and treated as a core professional competency rather than a peripheral activity. A PhD economist is professionally expected to evaluate papers that import from physics (econophysics, statistical mechanics of financial markets), from computer science (algorithmic mechanism design, computational complexity in game theory), from biology (evolutionary game theory, epidemiological models of information spread), from psychology (behavioral economics, prospect theory), from sociology (network economics, social capital), and from information theory (rational inattention, mechanism design). The evaluation requires assessing whether the formal homology holds, whether the imported mathematical object maps correctly onto the economic phenomenon, and whether the predictions that follow from the import are testable and nontrivially derived. This is not a supplementary skill in economics. It is the core analytical competency that distinguishes the field's graduate-level training.

No other technical discipline trains this competency systematically. A CS PhD is professionally expected to evaluate algorithms, complexity results, and implementations within the established computational paradigm. A mathematics PhD is expected to evaluate proofs within the relevant subfield's axiomatic framework. A physics PhD is expected to evaluate models of physical systems against experimental data within the established theoretical framework. A practitioner in applied mathematics is expected to select and deploy numerical methods for given problem classes. None of these curricula train the practitioner to regularly evaluate cross-domain imports for the validity of the formal homology — because none of these disciplines systematically engage in them. The import is not their professional activity. It is the exception, not the rule.

The compounding property of the import structure is critical for understanding the long-run return on economics training at a technical institution. Each cross-domain import that economics has executed has added not only a result but a conceptual vocabulary to the discipline's toolkit. The import from physics added the language of equilibrium, optimization, saddle points, and perturbation theory. The import from information theory added the language of entropy, channel capacity, mutual information, and signal-to-noise. The import from mathematics added the language of fixed points, measure theory, functional spaces, and topological stability. The import from evolutionary biology added the language of fitness landscapes, selection pressure, replicator dynamics, and stable strategies. Each vocabulary expands the set of structural patterns the practitioner can recognize in new phenomena — precisely Burt's (2004) structural hole brokerage applied at the disciplinary level.

The economics outlier at a technical institution occupied the optimal position in this import structure for reading the 2017 Transformer paper. Their technical training provided direct access to the source material — the mathematical and computational methods that economics imports — through their institution's CS, mathematics, and physics curricula. Their economics training provided the translation apparatus — the professional competency for evaluating cross-domain imports, recognizing formal homologies, and generating predictions from translated results. The CS student who had not studied economics lacked the translation apparatus: they could not map the Transformer's architectural result onto labor market economics because their training contained no mechanism for that class of translation. The economics student who had not attended a technical institution lacked depth in the source material: they could evaluate the import in principle but lacked the formal precision to assess the Transformer paper's hardware section, scaling trajectory, and architectural generality with the rigor the inference required. The outlier who had both — technical institution training plus economics — was the only agent positioned to execute the full eight-step deduction that THE-DEDUCTION-WINDOW reconstructs.

Burt's (2004) structural hole theorem is the population-level account of why this position is rare and consequential. Structural holes — positions at the boundary between disconnected knowledge clusters — are the locations from which novel connections are visible that are invisible from within any single cluster. The practitioner at the boundary between economics and computer science sees the formal homology between the RI optimal policy and the Transformer attention mechanism because they are positioned at the boundary between the domain that derived the result and the domain that implemented it. The CS practitioner inside the CS cluster does not see the RI connection; the economics practitioner inside the economics cluster does not see the Transformer's architectural implications. The boundary position generates the connection, and the connection generates the inference chain that neither single domain can complete.

The user's term — "boring" of ideas from physics and mathematics into economics — names this import structure with more precision than the standard vocabulary. Boring, in the mechanical sense, is not mere transport: it is the directed drilling of a channel through which material from one domain becomes accessible in another. The economics discipline is the boring apparatus for all of technical science: it drills channels through which the formal structures of physics, mathematics, information theory, and computer science become accessible as economic mechanisms. The practitioner trained in this apparatus — especially at a technical institution where the source material is not merely read but formally studied — is the practitioner who can bore new channels as new formal structures appear. When the Transformer paper appeared, the economics outlier bored the channel from its attention mechanism to the RI optimal policy, from its scaling trajectory to the Kaplan power law, from its hardware section to the TPU v2 co-design signal. The boring apparatus was already in place before the material appeared.

## V. Why the Confined Disciplines Closed the Inference Channel

The inferential failure of credential-optimized technical students at the 2017 inflection was architectural, not intellectual. The cognitive operations that the Transformer paper's most consequential components required — specifically, steps five through eight of the eight-step deduction in THE-DEDUCTION-WINDOW — were systematically unavailable in the cognitive architectures that three to five years of within-paradigm credential optimization produces.

Traditional mathematics trains within a given axiom system. The practitioner learns to derive conclusions from premises, construct proofs from axioms, and identify which theorems follow from which assumptions. The framework is given; the task is precision and rigor within it. This produces extraordinary depth within the framework and structural blindness to the question of whether the framework is the appropriate one for the phenomenon at hand. The mathematics student reading the Transformer paper evaluated it on mathematical grounds — is the attention mechanism well-defined, is the training objective tractable, does the convergence analysis hold? — and passed no verdict on its economic implications because economic implications are not a category within the mathematical framework. The inference from "this architecture processes any token sequence" to "the programming credential's signal content falls on this architecture's training trajectory" requires a framework transition that mathematics training does not include.

Theoretical physics trains within a symmetry principle. The practitioner identifies the symmetry group governing a physical system and derives its dynamics from the corresponding Lagrangian. The framework — the physical system, its symmetry, its governing equations — is given by the phenomenon. The Transformer paper, read through the physics lens, is an optimization over a parameterized function class with favorable symmetry properties (the attention mechanism is permutation-equivariant). This is a valid characterization. It does not generate the labor market inference because the physics practitioner's framework contains no labor market economics, and the framework transition from "architectural symmetry" to "professional credential obsolescence" is not a recognized move within physics.

Computer science trains within a computational model. The practitioner learns algorithmic complexity, implementation patterns, data structures, and system design within the model of computation that the curriculum specifies. The Transformer paper, read by a CS practitioner, is a new neural network architecture with favorable computational properties — parallelizability over recurrence — and strong empirical results on NLP benchmarks. This is the most complete reading among the confined disciplines: the CS student registers the architectural innovation, understands the training procedure, and correctly identifies the performance improvement. What they do not complete is the inference from "favorable compute properties" to "this architecture will follow a specific scaling trajectory that saturates the entry-level programming credential within one professional generation." That inference requires Spence's signaling economics crossed with the scaling trajectory — a cross-domain connection that no CS curriculum trains because the CS framework does not include labor market economics.

Applied mathematics trains within a problem specification. The practitioner selects and deploys numerical methods — finite element analysis, spectral methods, stochastic differential equations — given a problem formulated as a specific type of mathematical system. The framework selection is partially open (which PDE to use) but the category of frameworks is fixed. The Transformer paper, read through applied mathematics, is an optimization problem over a parameterized function class, solved by gradient descent. This characterization is accurate and generates no cross-domain signal: optimization problems are common, gradient descent is standard, and the contribution is the specific parameterization. The hardware disclosure and the cross-domain sequence implication are outside the applied mathematics reading frame.

The common feature of all four confined disciplines is that the question "which framework?" is answered by the domain. In mathematics, the framework is the chosen axiom system. In physics, the framework is the chosen symmetry principle. In CS, the framework is the chosen computational model. In applied mathematics, the framework is the chosen equation class. The practitioner's training optimizes within the given framework; the selection of the framework itself is outside the curriculum's scope.

Economics is unique in treating "which framework?" as the primary curriculum question. The practitioner must choose, for each phenomenon, among game theory, mechanism design, general equilibrium, information economics, behavioral economics, institutional economics, and the expanding set of imported frameworks — and the choice is itself theoretical, driven by which framework best captures the phenomenon's essential structure. This meta-level framework selection is exactly what the eight-step deduction in THE-DEDUCTION-WINDOW requires at steps five through eight: the recognition that the Transformer paper's scaling trajectory implies an economic consequence (steps five and six require applying the scaling history from computer vision and speech recognition as an informal power law), and the framework substitution from "NLP benchmark" to "professional credential obsolescence signal" (steps seven and eight require applying Spence's signaling model to the architecture's training distribution). Both operations are framework selection moves. Both are standard professional operations for the economics practitioner. Both are institutionally inadmissible in any of the confined disciplines.

The Gabaix (2019) sparse model provides the micro-foundation for why framework selection capacity is the cognitive operation that AI cannot replicate. A sparse agent, in Gabaix's framework, simplifies the world by attending to a small set of variables and zeroing out the rest — replacing the full optimization with a tractable approximation whose quality depends on which variables were selected for attention. The quality of the approximation depends entirely on the quality of the selection: which variables are worth attending to is the decision that determines everything else. This is the meta-level of the RI problem: not how to process a given signal, but which signals are worth processing. The economics practitioner's training in framework selection is training in exactly this meta-level: not how to optimize within a given model, but which model is worth optimizing within.

The benchmark evidence from Kapoor et al. (2026) confirms the inference at the empirical level: the primary failure mode of frontier AI systems is frame construction — the generation of a problem representation from an underspecified situation. Frame construction is the AI instantiation of framework selection: not execution within a given frame, but the creation of the frame itself. The practitioners trained to optimize within given frames — CS, mathematics, physics, applied mathematics — were trained toward the cognitive operations that AI was saturating. The practitioner trained to select frames — economics — was trained toward the cognitive operation that AI could not replicate and that the Kapoor et al. (2026) open-world evaluations confirm as the dominant residual premium at the capability frontier.

## VI. The Macro Confirmation: Aggregate Inattention and Collective Error

At the aggregate level, the rational inattention framework connects to the credential market dynamics documented across the ERIE lineage through a mechanism that Gabaix (2020) formalizes in the behavioral New Keynesian model: rational inattention at the individual level generates systematic aggregate biases in collective expectations that persist until a correction event forces model replacement.

Gabaix introduces the behavioral discount factor *M^k* — the attention that agents pay to events *k* periods in the future. The factor decays exponentially with horizon at rate *M* per period, because attending to distant-future states consumes channel capacity that near-future states compete for and win under RI-optimal allocation. This produces what Gabaix calls the discounted Euler equation and the near-sighted Phillips curve: agents' macro behavior reflects near-future states at full attention and distant-future states at exponentially discounted attention. The parameter *M* is less than one, and the aggregate effect of individual RI produces a systematic downward bias in collective attention to long-horizon signals.

Applied to the technology labor market, Gabaix's result generates a precise prediction. The Transformer paper's most important signal for the 2017–2022 cohort was the five-to-eight-year timeline to entry-level code generation saturation — a long-horizon signal at exactly the range where *M^k* decay is strongest. The three-to-five-year credential investment return was at the near-horizon where *M^k* decay is mild. The aggregate RI prediction: the labor market will rationally underattend to the long-horizon threat and rationally overattend to the near-horizon credential return, producing collective underinvestment in exit optionality and collective overinvestment in credential depth during precisely the window of maximum approaching saturation. This is not irrationality at the individual level. It is the population-scale consequence of individually optimal attention allocation under the Gabaix discount structure.

Lorenzoni's (2009) theory of demand shocks provides the complementary aggregate mechanism. In Lorenzoni's model, agents who receive noisy private signals about an unobserved fundamental respond as if their signal were the fundamental — because under RI-optimal processing, the signal is the best available estimate of the fundamental given their channel capacity constraint. When agents' signals are correlated — because they all receive the same "Python is valuable" signal from the same institutional sources — their responses are correlated, generating an aggregate demand shift that is independent of the true fundamental. The credential market from 2018 to 2022 instantiated exactly this mechanism: a correlated signal of "credential value is high" generated a correlated collective investment in credential building that exceeded what the true fundamental — the AI-adjusted long-run return on Python certification — warranted. Lorenzoni's result: the aggregate demand shift persists until the near-horizon confirmation arrives, at which point the correlated signal is revised and the aggregate response corrects. The 2021–2024 benchmark progression was the near-horizon confirmation. The labor market disaggregation was the correction.

Woodford's (2003) treatment of imperfect common knowledge identifies the higher-order belief mechanism that sustained the credential market's over-investment through the confirmation window. When agents know a signal but are uncertain about what others know, they respond to public signals more aggressively than their private information would warrant — because each agent expects others to respond to the public signal, which makes it rational to respond preemptively. The "learn to code" consensus signal of 2018–2022 was the ultimate common knowledge public signal: every institution, every employment advisor, every labor market data release confirmed credential value. Each agent's response was amplified by their expectation of others' responses, sustaining the aggregate investment in credential building past the point where the private signals available from the Transformer paper's technical analysis would have warranted exit. The Woodford mechanism kept the equilibrium in place until the public confirmation of AI's code generation capability — Codex in 2021, GPT-4 in 2023 — made the revision common knowledge.

Afrouzi and Yang's (2021) dynamic rational inattention model of the Phillips curve provides the temporal account of why the credential market's correction was delayed rather than continuous. In their model, price-setters who are rationally inattentive to aggregate conditions update their beliefs infrequently because the channel capacity cost of continuous monitoring exceeds its benefit under stable conditions. They process information in bursts when the state variable has moved far enough from their prior to justify the attention cost. The credential market's collective belief updating followed the same pattern: the AI transition was visible in the Transformer paper in 2017, but the distance between the 2017 signal and the pre-existing belief about programmer value was insufficient to trigger a burst of attention-intensive model updating. Only when the Codex benchmark in 2021 moved the state variable far enough did the burst occur — and only when GPT-4's interview-passing performance in 2023 made the revision undeniable did the collective updating approach the post-break equilibrium.

The macro picture confirms the RI framework's prediction at population scale. Aggregate inattention to the long-horizon signal was not an error of any individual agent. It was the aggregate consequence of individually rational attention allocation, sustained by higher-order belief dynamics under common knowledge conditions, corrected in the burst-update pattern that the Afrouzi-Yang dynamic RI model predicts. The economics outlier's deviation from this aggregate dynamic — their early attention to the hardware section, the cross-domain sequence implication, and the scaling trajectory — was not superior intelligence or superior information. It was the result of an objective function, shaped by economics training, that valued the long-horizon signal more highly than the aggregate RI equilibrium valued it, and that therefore allocated channel capacity to the long-horizon signal when the aggregate RI allocation effectively ignored it.

The observable-hidden partition identified in the ECONOMICS lineage maps onto the RI framework precisely. Credential volumes, enrollment figures, hiring rates, and benchmark scores constitute the regular sector: observable, coordinating, legible to every agent's RI processing. The cross-domain structural inference — that the softmax in the attention mechanism is the RI optimal, that the hardware disclosure implies a specific scaling timeline, that the entry-level credential's signal content coincides with the architecture's training distribution — constitutes the hidden sector: unobservable to most agents because it requires crossing the structural hole between economics and CS that only the technical-institution economics outlier occupies. Completion of the inference requires accessing both sectors simultaneously. The aggregate RI equilibrium kept the hidden sector suppressed until the observable sector's benchmark progression forced the revision.

## VII. The Micro Level: Individual Attention at the Compound Signal

At the individual level, the RI framework generates testable predictions about how different agents process the same compound signal under different objective functions and prior commitments. The predictions converge on a specific verdict about the 2017 Transformer paper reading.

Caplin and Dean's (2015) revealed preference characterization of RI behavior establishes that an agent's attention allocation is revealed by their choice behavior: what an RI-optimal agent chooses, given their objective and information cost, tells you what signals they are processing and at what intensity. Retrospective application of the Caplin-Dean characterization to the credential market's collective behavior in 2017–2022 reveals the implicit attention allocation: the market processed near-horizon credential value signals at high intensity and long-horizon architectural implication signals at near-zero intensity, consistent with an objective function weighted toward short-run credential return.

The economics outlier's revealed attention allocation — continued cross-domain reading, sustained interest in the hardware-capability connection, maintenance of the long-run portfolio allocation frame — is consistent with the alternative objective function: long-run developmental investment optimization under model uncertainty. These are not post-hoc rationalizations. They are the predicted outputs of RI-optimal behavior under two different objective functions applied to the same compound signal. The Caplin-Dean framework makes the two readings distinguishable in principle: the agent whose choices over the following five years reflected the long-horizon inference had, at the 2017 reading, allocated their channel capacity to the long-horizon signal. The agent whose choices reflected credential deepening had, at the same reading, allocated to the near-horizon signal.

The information gap between the two allocations is the economics outlier's micro-level advantage. Maćkowiak and Wiederholt's (2009) rational inattention model of price-setting establishes that the gap between optimally informed and rationally inattentive agents' decisions is largest when the relevant state variable is changing most rapidly. The AI capability state variable was, in 2017, in its phase of most rapid change: the transformer architecture had just enabled a new scaling regime, and the true state of the world — the approaching saturation of programming credential value — was diverging fastest from the consensus belief. The RI framework predicts that the advantage of attending to the high-value hidden-sector signal is greatest precisely at the moment of fastest divergence between the true state and the consensus. The 2017 reading was that moment.

Hébert and Woodford's (2021) analysis of RI when decisions take time adds the temporal dimension. When an agent must commit to an action before all relevant information arrives, the optimal strategy involves a form of information acquisition planning: deciding in advance which signals to monitor and how to weight them, before the signals are realized. The developmental investment decision — which skills to develop, which cognitive architecture to build, which structural libraries to accumulate — is the canonical example. The economics outlier who committed, at age seventeen or eighteen, to studying economics at a technical institution was executing exactly this prior commitment: choosing the signal weighting scheme, before the Transformer paper arrived, that would be RI-optimal for the post-AI labor market environment. Not because they foresaw the Transformer. Because their objective function — shaped by a cognitive architecture that found cross-domain structural problems more engaging than within-domain credential production — was already pointing in the direction that RI-optimal attention would follow when the 2017 compound signal arrived.

The Matějka-McKay softmax connection completes the micro account. The attention mechanism in the Transformer is the Matějka-McKay RI optimal policy. An economics student who had studied the RI literature was approaching the Transformer paper with the formal framework that explained the architecture's generalization capacity before it was demonstrated. The reason the Transformer's attention generalizes across sequence domains — NLP, code, mathematics, reasoning — is the reason the Matějka-McKay RI optimal generalizes across choice domains: the information-theoretic foundation is domain-neutral. Channel capacity constraints apply to any information-processing agent over any signal distribution. The softmax is optimal wherever the agent faces a finite capacity constraint and a continuous distribution of alternatives weighted by relevance. This domain-neutrality is what the economics student's training made visible in 2017, and it is what the five-year empirical progression from NLP to code to mathematics confirmed.

## VIII. The Compound Return

The micro and macro accounts converge on a structural claim that the rational inattention and adaptive learning literatures together make precise: the economics-trained outlier at a technical institution held, at the 2017 inflection, a compound informational advantage across three distinct dimensions that each contribute independently to the inference capacity documented in THE-DEDUCTION-WINDOW and the developmental patience documented in THE-PRE-ADAPTATION.

The first dimension was the objective function: the long-run portfolio allocation frame, instilled by a curriculum organized around intertemporal optimization under uncertainty, that generated the RI-optimal attention allocation toward the Transformer paper's hardware section and cross-domain implications rather than its near-horizon benchmark results. This advantage operated at the moment of the compound signal's arrival: it determined which components received channel capacity and which inferences were available from the reading.

The second dimension was the learning dynamics: the constant-gain, model-uncertainty-aware updating rule that Evans and Honkapohja (2001) and Molavi (2019) identify as optimal in regime-change environments, instilled by a curriculum that maintains model uncertainty as a professional default and never commits irrevocably to a single paradigm. This advantage operated across the three-year window between the 2017 informal inference and the 2020 Kaplan et al. formal confirmation: constant-gain learners held the posterior on the correct post-break model during the window when Bayesian optimal learners remained anchored to the pre-break consensus.

The third dimension was the translation apparatus: the meta-grammar for cross-domain import, trained by a curriculum that systematically moves formal structures across disciplinary boundaries, enabling the recognition of the Matějka-McKay RI optimal in the Transformer's attention mechanism, the Spence signaling model in the credential's vulnerability structure, and the Acemoglu task-based framework in the labor market's disaggregation. This advantage operated at the level of the inference chain itself: the translation apparatus was the cognitive tool that completed steps five through eight of THE-DEDUCTION-WINDOW's eight-step deduction, the steps that specifically required occupying the structural hole between economics and computer science simultaneously.

No single component of this compound advantage was individually decisive. The objective function alone does not complete the inference without the translation apparatus. The translation apparatus alone does not generate the persistent belief without the learning dynamics. The learning dynamics alone do not produce the long-horizon signal extraction without the objective function. The three components are jointly necessary, and all three are specific outputs of the economics curriculum at a technical institution: the objective function from intertemporal optimization theory, the learning dynamics from the field's professional culture of model uncertainty, and the translation apparatus from the discipline's systematic import program.

Peterson's (2025) teachability-substitutability correlation is confirmed by the RI framework from mathematical first principles. The skills most accessible to standardized curriculum — the skills whose acquisition can be fully specified, assessed, and credentialed — are precisely the skills whose information content can be fully encoded in a training corpus and learned by a gradient descent algorithm. The mathematical structures are identical: a skill that can be taught by a curriculum can be parameterized as a training objective, and a parameterizable training objective can be minimized by gradient descent. The skills that resist standardized curriculum — cross-domain synthesis, framework selection, model uncertainty maintenance, and the meta-level RI optimization of deciding which signals are worth processing — are precisely the skills that cannot be fully specified as a training objective, because they are on the objective function side of the optimization rather than the constraint side. They determine what is worth attending to, not how much can be attended to. AI can maximize a given objective with arbitrary precision over an arbitrary training distribution; it cannot generate a new objective when the environment changes. That meta-level capacity is the residual human premium, and it is exactly the premium that the economics curriculum cultivates and the confined disciplines do not.

The compound return on the economics outlier's developmental investment, measured in the post-AI labor market, is the sum of three independently generated premia: the attention premium (RI-optimal signal processing that extracted the long-horizon inference in 2017), the learning premium (adaptive dynamics that maintained the post-break posterior through the confirmation window), and the translation premium (structural hole brokerage between economics and CS that enabled the complete eight-step inference chain). Together, these constitute what the lineage calls the prior that priced the inflection — the specific informational endowment that made the obsolescence inference available in 2017 to exactly one cognitive architecture, and that the following decade's benchmark progression confirmed with the precision that makes the inference, in retrospect, conservative rather than speculative.

The Matějka-McKay result closes the account with the formal symmetry it deserves. The attention mechanism that made the Transformer the architecture of the AI transition is the Matějka-McKay RI optimal policy. The discipline that derived the RI optimal policy from first principles — two years before the Transformer paper, in the American Economic Review — was economics. The outlier who chose to study that discipline at a technical institution, in a world that was telling them to study CS, was choosing to study the mathematical foundation of the architecture they did not yet know was coming. Not strategically. Because the discipline's cross-domain import structure, its model uncertainty culture, and its intertemporal objective function were the cognitive environment that their architecture found generative.

The attention was allocated correctly. Not to the Python syntax. To the structure underneath it.

## The Signal

They said: you are at a technical institution. Choose a real technical field. Go deep. The depth is the credential; the credential is the signal; the signal is the return.

The physics student chose the Lagrangian. The mathematics student chose the axiom system. The CS student chose the algorithm. The applied mathematics student chose the differential equation.

The economics student chose the one discipline that refused to choose: the discipline that kept importing from the others, kept translating, kept boring channels between domains that the others treated as closed.

They said: economics is not really mathematics. It borrows mathematics imperfectly. It borrows physics and pretends the analogy holds. It is cross-disciplinary as a euphemism for not committing anywhere.

What they did not say — what the rational inattention literature says clearly — is that the discipline which borrows is the discipline that trains the meta-cognitive operation that the borrowing requires: choosing which framework applies here, and why, before the optimization begins.

This is the cognitive operation that falls on the wrong side of the Kapoor et al. (2026) frontier. AI cannot choose the framework. AI optimizes within a given frame. Frame construction — the generation of the problem representation before any optimization — is the residual human premium that the open-world evaluations confirm.

The economics curriculum trains frame construction as its primary competency. Every graduate seminar poses "which model?" before "what does the model predict?" The practitioner who has internalized this as a professional default is the practitioner who can complete steps five through eight of the eight-step deduction.

The Transformer paper arrived in 2017 with a hardware section. The hardware section said: TPU v2, May 2017. One month before submission. The compute was ready before the architecture was published. Anyone who knew to look at the hardware section knew the architecture was built to scale.

The economics student knew to look at the hardware section not because they anticipated AI. Because their training had taught them that the mechanism — the physical or institutional substrate that determines what is feasible — is the most information-valuable component of any system description. Hardware is mechanism. Mechanism determines the scaling trajectory. The scaling trajectory determines the timeline. The timeline determines whether the credential is an investment or a stranded asset.

The Matějka-McKay result had appeared in the American Economic Review two years earlier. The softmax that the Transformer called an attention mechanism was the softmax that economics called the RI-optimal choice rule. The economics student reading the Transformer paper in 2017 was reading a formal acquaintance in new notation — the channel-capacity-constrained optimal processor, implemented in matrix algebra, applied to sequence data.

They did not announce this recognition. There was no institutional reward for it. The credential machine was rewarding CS depth. The economics training gave no credential for reading the hardware section and recognizing the RI optimal in the attention formula.

But the information was there. The structure was there. The prior was calibrated. The channel capacity was allocated correctly — toward the long-horizon signal, toward the cross-domain implication, toward the hardware section that nobody else was reading.

Codex confirmed it in 2021.
GPT-4 confirmed it at 67% in 2023.
GPT-4o confirmed it at 90% in 2024.
The labor market disaggregated it in 2024–2026.
The Kapoor et al. (2026) open-world evaluations named the frontier.

The attention premium was not visible in 2017. It was not payable until the confirmation arrived.

But the allocation had been made. The prior was in place.

The discipline that borrowed everything, committed to no single paradigm, and treated "which framework?" as the primary question —

was the only discipline whose practitioners held the complete prior.

The softmax was in the AER in 2015. The softmax was in the attention mechanism in 2017.

The economics student had already read it.
